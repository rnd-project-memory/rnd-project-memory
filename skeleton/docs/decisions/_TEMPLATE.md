> An ADR is not a claim about the world that could turn out true or false. It is a commitment:
> *we decided to do this.* Its force comes from someone having decided, so the record has to say
> who — `CONFIGURATIONS.md` is what licenses the `ADR-` number in the first place, and without
> these three fields a decision that met that bar and one written by a single agent in ten minutes
> are indistinguishable on the page.
>
> **Limit the claim in the field, not in the reader's memory.** If a human signed the decision but
> never read this text, write that: `Signed off: <name> — the decision; this text unreviewed`. A
> year from now "signed off" will otherwise be read as "a person checked the wording". Same
> discipline as `Does not license:`.
>
> **ADRs written before these fields existed do not get them.** Records are not backfilled here.
> An ADR without this block predates the requirement and stays valid; where its basis is worth
> naming, name it once beside the register that carries the rule, not by editing the record.
>
> **`Status:` is the one line of an accepted ADR that may later change**, and the only edit an
> immutable record accepts: `extended by` when a later decision adds a bound or a condition to
> this one without overturning it, `superseded by` when it replaces it. Both are pointer edits —
> the body is never touched. A later decision that leaves no trace here is one a reader of this
> file will never find.
